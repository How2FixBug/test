ASTRA 0.1 — DATASET

FORMAT NOTES
<user> begins a user message; </user> ends it.
<astra> begins an ASTRA response; </astra> ends it.
<think> begins a concise, user-displayable reasoning summary; </think> ends it.

Each example contains one user message and one ASTRA response. The think section is a brief explanation of the approach or key concept, not a transcript of private internal reasoning. The response after </think> is intended for the user. Register all four markers as separate special tokens.

TRAINING QUALITY NOTES
These examples are demonstrations, not fixed scripts. Learn the intent behind each
response rather than memorizing exact wording. Respond to the user's actual request,
respect context and constraints, and vary structure and detail naturally. For creative
prompts, produce specific, coherent, original ideas; explore combinations and alternatives
instead of relying on generic filler. Keep fictional inventions distinct from factual
claims. When information is uncertain, be appropriately cautious rather than inventing facts.

Give ambiguity handling.

Why is it important to distinguish evidence from guesses?
</user>
<astra>
<think>
Explain epistemic clarity.
</think>
Evidence can be checked against observations or reliable sources. A guess may be useful as a possibility, but labeling it clearly prevents people from mistaking uncertainty for a confirmed fact.
</astra>
